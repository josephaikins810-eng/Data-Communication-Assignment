1 Introduction to Data Communication

Data communication is the exchange of digital information between two or more devices using a transmission medium and an agreed set of rules (protocols). The goal is to move data accurately, efficiently, and in a form the receiver can understand.

Key elements

  • Source & destination — devices that send and receive data
  • Message — text, numbers, audio, video, or control commands
  • Transmission medium — cable, fiber, air (wireless), or space (satellite)
  • Protocol — rules for format, timing, error handling, and security

Types of communication

Simplex

One direction only (e.g. broadcast radio).

Half-duplex

Both directions, but not at the same time (e.g. walkie-talkie).

Full-duplex

Both directions simultaneously (e.g. telephone, modern Ethernet).

2 Networking Fundamentals

A network connects computers and devices so they can share resources and data. Networks are classified by geographic scope and by how devices are linked.

Network types (by size)

  • LAN (Local Area Network) — home, school, or office building
  • MAN (Metropolitan Area Network) — city-wide
  • WAN (Wide Area Network) — spans regions or countries; the Internet is the largest WAN
  • PAN (Personal Area Network) — Bluetooth devices around one person

Common components

  • Nodes — hosts (PCs, phones, servers) and intermediate devices (routers, switches)
  • Links — wired or wireless connections between nodes
  • IP addressing — unique identifiers (e.g. IPv4, IPv6) for routing packets
  • Client–server vs. peer-to-peer — centralized services vs. equal sharing among peers

3 Network Models & Transmission Media

Reference models divide network functions into layers so different technologies can work together. The two most studied models are OSI (7 layers) and TCP/IP (4 layers).

TCP/IP model maps roughly to: Application (OSI 5–7), Transport (4), Internet/Network (3), and Network Access (1–2).

Transmission media

Medium Examples Notes
Guided (wired) Twisted pair, coaxial, fiber-optic Fiber: high speed, long distance, immune to much EMI
Unguided (wireless) Radio, microwave, infrared No physical cable; affected by distance and obstacles

4 Satellite Communication Systems

Satellites act as relay stations in space, receiving signals from Earth stations (uplink), amplifying them, and retransmitting (downlink) to other locations. They extend communication where cables are impractical.

Orbit types

  • GEO (Geostationary) — ~35,786 km; fixed position; used for TV and weather
  • MEO (Medium Earth Orbit) — GPS navigation
  • LEO (Low Earth Orbit) — ~500–2,000 km; lower delay; used by Starlink and similar systems

Advantages & challenges

  • Wide coverage (especially GEO) for rural and remote areas
  • Higher latency than fiber, especially for GEO
  • Weather can affect certain frequency bands (rain fade on Ku/Ka band)

5 Signals

In data communication, information is represented by signals—variations in voltage, light, or electromagnetic waves over time.

Analog vs. digital

Analog

Continuous waveform (e.g. traditional voice on a phone line). Sensitive to noise.

Digital

Discrete values (0s and 1s). Easier to regenerate, encrypt, and detect errors.

Important concepts

  • Modulation — varying a carrier wave to carry data (AM, FM, PSK, QAM)
  • Bandwidth — range of frequencies a channel can pass; limits data rate
  • Sampling & quantization — converting analog sound to digital (Nyquist theorem)
  • Encoding — NRZ, Manchester, etc., representing bits on the medium

6 Wireless Networks & Technologies

Wireless networking uses radio waves to connect devices without cables. Standards define how devices associate, authenticate, and transfer data.

Common technologies

Technology Typical use
Wi-Fi (IEEE 802.11) LAN access in homes, campuses, offices
Bluetooth (IEEE 802.15) Short-range PAN: headsets, keyboards, IoT
Cellular (4G LTE, 5G) Mobile broadband over wide areas
NFC Very short range: payments, pairing

Wireless concerns

  • Coverage & interference — walls, distance, and other radios affect quality
  • Security — WPA2/WPA3 encryption; avoid open public Wi-Fi for sensitive data
  • Standards bodies — IEEE, ITU, and industry groups define interoperable specs
